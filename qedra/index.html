Qedra

A university mathematics solver that shows its working — and tells you what it assumed. Exact arithmetic, no rounding, and it runs entirely on your device. iPhone and iPad.

About

Qedra solves calculus, algebra and linear algebra problems and writes out the derivation the way a lecturer would: one named rule at a time, with a short explanation of why that step is allowed. It is built on a computer algebra system written from scratch for this app, which means two things you will notice immediately.

  • The arithmetic is exact. Fractions stay fractions and integers stay whole, however large. A determinant that is 1 comes back as 1, not 0.9999999999999998.
  • It works offline. There is no server. Turn on Airplane Mode and it still solves — your problems never leave the device.

Getting started

  1. Type a problem into the box — for example d/dx x^x, integral of t^5/(t^6-6)^3 dt, or det([[1,2],[3,4]]).
  2. Tap Solve. The answer appears first, with a badge saying whether Qedra could verify it.
  3. Read down for the worked solution. Tap any step to expand the reasoning behind it.

If your input could mean more than one thing, Qedra says so above the answer and offers the other readings — it does not guess silently.

What it can solve

  • Calculus. Derivatives including the chain, product and quotient rules and logarithmic differentiation; indefinite and definite integrals by table, substitution, partial fractions and integration by parts, including the cyclic cases; limits, including every indeterminate form — 0/0, ∞/∞, 0·∞, ∞−∞, 1∞, 00 and ∞0.
  • Algebra. Simplification, expansion, factoring, and equations — linear, quadratic, polynomial, rational, and the trigonometric ones whose answer is an infinite family rather than a number.
  • Linear algebra. Exact determinants, reduced row echelon form with every row operation shown, rank, matrix inverse, and systems of linear equations — including the ones with infinitely many solutions, written out as a parametrised family.

Why the answers say “assuming”

Some steps are only valid under a condition. Simplifying √(x²) to x is wrong when x is negative; dividing by a symbol is wrong when that symbol is zero. Most solvers do these silently. Qedra records the assumption and shows it with the answer, so you can check whether it holds for your problem. If you are working over the complex numbers, turn that on in Settings and Qedra will stop making the assumptions that only hold over the reals.

Why it sometimes refuses

Qedra will tell you it could not finish, and what it tried. This is deliberate. A search that gives up and prints a plausible-looking answer is worse than useless for checking your own work, so when the engine cannot prove a result it says so rather than guessing. The same applies to verification: an answer marked “could not verify” is not necessarily wrong, and Qedra says which of the two it means.

Notation settings

Some notation genuinely changes the answer, so Qedra asks rather than assuming. log(100) is 2 in base 10 and about 4.605 as a natural logarithm. Set your convention under Settings → Notation; it applies to what you solve next, and work already in your history keeps the convention it was solved under.

FAQ

Do my problems get uploaded anywhere?

No. The solver is built into the app and runs on your device. There is no server to send anything to. The only things that leave your device are advertising requests on the free tier, purchases (handled by Apple), and your history if you have iCloud switched on — which goes to your own private iCloud, not to us.

Is it free?

Answers are free and unlimited, permanently. Three full worked solutions a day are free as well. Pro removes that daily limit.

Why does it show a different form of the answer than my textbook?

There is usually more than one correct way to write the same expression. Qedra prefers the form a lecturer would write — coefficients first, then algebra, then named functions — but an answer that looks different is often the same expression rearranged. If you think it is genuinely wrong, please send it to us; a wrong answer is a bug we want.

Can it take a photo of my homework?

Not yet. Qedra takes typed input today. Camera input is planned.

Does it do statistics or differential equations?

Not yet — those are being built. Today it covers calculus, algebra and linear algebra as listed above.

Does it work on iPad?

Yes, on iPhone and iPad, with the layout adapting to the size of the screen.

I restored my purchase and it did not appear.

Purchases are tied to your Apple Account. Make sure the device is signed in with the same Apple Account you bought with, then use Settings → Membership → Restore Purchases. If it still does not appear, email us.

I found a wrong answer, a bug, or want a feature.

Please tell us, and include the exact problem you typed. A wrong mathematical result is the most serious kind of bug this app can have and it will be treated that way.
