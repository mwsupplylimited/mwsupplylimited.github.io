Roadbook GPS

A simple, private trip logger for iPhone. Version 1.0

About

Roadbook GPS turns your iPhone into a personal trip logger. It records the route, distance, duration, speed and elevation of every drive, can start and stop itself automatically when you set off and arrive, and keeps a bird's-eye view of everywhere you've ever driven. Everything stays on your device, or in your own iCloud account. No accounts, no advertising, no tracking.

Getting started

  • Grant location "While Using" the first time you open the app to record a drive manually. To use auto‑start, grant "Allow Always" instead — iOS needs that to wake the app when you start driving.
  • Tap Start on the Record tab to begin recording. The big number is your live speed; the grid below tracks distance, duration, altitude, max and average speed, and elevation gain.
  • Leave the app or lock the phone — recording keeps going. Roadbook GPS records in the background.
  • Auto‑start & auto‑stop can be toggled and tuned in Settings. By default, recording starts when you exceed a threshold for two consecutive samples and stops after sitting below the stop speed for a few seconds.
  • Trips tab lists every saved drive. Tap one to see the route on a map and full stats, and to export it as GPX or JSON.
  • Explore tab shows every trip overlaid on a single world map plus a list of countries and cities you've driven through.

iCloud Backup

If you are signed in to iCloud and have iCloud Drive enabled, your trips are stored in your own iCloud Documents container (iCloud.com.edfd.DrivingRecorder) and follow you across your iPhones and iPads automatically. There is no separate account or password — iOS uses your Apple ID. You can see the current backup state and force a refresh from Settings → iCloud Backup inside the app. If iCloud is not available, trips are kept on the device.

FAQ

Why is altitude / elevation gain showing 0?

iPhone altitude comes from a combination of GPS and the device's built-in barometer. The iOS Simulator has no barometer and reports only latitude and longitude, so altitude reads 0 there. On a real iPhone outdoors, altitude populates within a few seconds of the first GPS fix.

Why is my average speed lower than I expected?

Average speed is computed over elapsed recording time, including any time you spent stationary (red lights, traffic). It's never higher than your max speed by construction.

Auto‑start didn't fire when I started driving.

Auto‑start needs the location permission set to Always, not just While Using. iOS uses significant-location-change monitoring to wake the app, which can take a minute or two after you start moving. You can also tune the start threshold in Settings, or just tap Start manually.

Where is my trip data sent?

Nowhere we control. Trips live on your device, and (if enabled) inside your personal iCloud account — we never receive them. See the Privacy Policy for details.

How do I get my trips off the phone?

Open a trip from the Trips tab and use Export GPX (for mapping tools like Google Earth, Strava, RideWithGPS) or Export JSON (for raw data). Either option opens the standard iOS share sheet, so you can send to Files, Mail, AirDrop, or any compatible app.

Will it drain my battery?

While recording, the app uses GPS continuously, which is energy-intensive. When you're not driving, the only background work is significant-location-change monitoring (only if auto‑start is on), which is designed by Apple to be very low power.
