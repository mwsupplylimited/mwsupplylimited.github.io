What it does

Wakeward is a wake-up alarm for iPhone and iPad built on AlarmKit, the framework Apple introduced in iOS 26 that gives an app the same alerting path as the built-in Clock. That means a Wakeward alarm sounds at alarm volume through Silent mode, through every Focus including Sleep, over the Lock Screen, and after a restart — and it keeps working if you force-quit the app or never open it again, because iOS itself is holding the alarm, not Wakeward.

On top of that it adds the three things the built-in Clock has no answer for.

The three differences

1. A backup alarm

A second, completely independent system alarm a few minutes after the first, armed at the same moment and cancelled automatically the instant you properly stop the first one. If anything at all stops you hearing the primary, the backup still rings.

It is also the honest answer to alarm collisions. When two apps have an alarm set for the same minute, iOS picks a winner non-deterministically and a single press of a physical button silences all currently ringing alarms. A backup three minutes later has nothing to compete with.

2. A bedtime check

One screen that verifies everything iOS will actually report: that permission is granted, that every alarm you have switched on is genuinely present in the system's own list (and re-arms it if not), that your audio is not routed to headphones, your battery is not about to die, whether you have changed time zone, and whether a daylight-saving change lands tonight.

It also asks you about the four things iOS refuses to tell any app — ringer volume, “Change with Buttons”, Attention Aware Features, and whether the app is hidden. Wakeward asks rather than guessing, because an advisory that says “you are muted” when you are not would destroy the point of the whole feature.

3. A receipt

After every alarm: when it was scheduled, when it actually rang, the drift between the two, when you stopped it, and the iOS build it happened on. Where iOS does not report something, the receipt says so rather than inventing a number.

Things worth knowing

Wakeward cannot control the alarm volume, and neither can any other app. iOS plays alarms at the system ringer volume and offers no API to change it. What Wakeward does control is how loudly its tones are mastered, and every one is built on the 520 Hz low-frequency signal that fire-safety standards mandate for sleeping areas precisely because it wakes people who sleep through higher-pitched sounds.

Do not hide Wakeward or lock it behind Face ID

Apple states plainly that alarms scheduled by hidden or passcode-locked apps silently fail. No app can detect this, so it is genuinely down to you.

Attention Aware Features lowers your alarm

If your iPhone's front camera sees you glance at it, iOS quietly reduces the alarm volume. Apps cannot detect or prevent this. You can switch it off in Settings › Face ID & Passcode › Attention Aware Features.

A stuck bubble in the Dynamic Island

There is a known iOS 26 bug where swiping an alarm away while the phone is unlocked can leave an empty Live Activity stuck in the Dynamic Island. It survives force-quitting and even deleting the app; restarting your iPhone clears it. This affects Apple's own sample code too — it is not something Wakeward can fix.

Requires iOS 26.2 or later

Earlier versions of iOS 26 have bugs in AlarmKit that stop custom alarm sounds playing or looping correctly. Rather than ship a wake-up alarm on top of those, Wakeward requires 26.2.

Common questions

Will it ring if I force-quit the app?

Yes. iOS holds the alarm, not Wakeward. It also survives a restart.

Will it ring if my phone is on silent?

Yes. Alarms scheduled through AlarmKit sound through the Ring/Silent switch and through every Focus, including Sleep.

Can I use it alongside the built-in Clock?

You can, but avoid setting both for the same minute — iOS picks between them unpredictably. If you keep Clock alarms, Wakeward's backup alarm is the safety net.

Do my alarms sync between my iPhone and iPad?

The alarm list syncs through your own iCloud. Whether a given device actually rings is a separate per-device switch, off by default on iPad, so an iPad on the kitchen counter does not join in at 6:30.

Does Wakeward track my sleep?

No. It records when your alarms rang and when you stopped them, and computes how consistent your wake time is from that. It does not use the microphone or the accelerometer to guess at sleep stages, because phone-derived sleep stages do not stand up to the evidence.
