About

Blackout Camera is built for dark environments — night-sky sessions, observatories, wildlife hides, and other places where a bright camera preview gets in the way. Take photos and record video for free. Blackout Pro adds recording with a black preview, manual controls, and creative tools including Time-lapse and Astro. There is also a red interface for framing and adjusting a shot with less screen glow. See Plans & ads for what each plan includes.

The big idea: Press Screen Off to hide the camera preview and lower display brightness while the app stays open. With Pro or an existing Basic subscription, video can keep recording behind the black preview. To bring the controls back, press and hold anywhere for about a second. Apple’s camera and microphone privacy indicators remain visible.

Getting started

  • Allow camera access when prompted. Microphone access is needed to include sound in video; the app explains this permission before recording.
  • Choose PHOTO or VIDEO with the selector above the shutter.
  • Tap the shutter to take a photo or start/stop recording.
  • Tap Screen Off to hide the preview. This is free while idle; keeping it black during a recording requires Pro or an existing Basic subscription.
  • Your photos and videos are saved to Apple Photos, to the app’s own in-app Library, to the Private Folder on Pro, or to any combination of them — you tick the ones you want in Settings → Saving & Privacy → Save to, and at least one always stays on.
  • Tap the thumbnail in the bottom-left corner to open the in-app Library. It shows captures saved to the In-App Library; open Apple Photos separately for Photos-only captures, or authenticate to open the Private Folder.
  • Add the Home Screen widget or a supported Control Center / Lock Screen control to start recording with your Quick Record settings. Permissions, plan access and device capabilities still apply.

The black screen

Turning it on

Tap Screen Off to replace the camera preview with black and reduce screen brightness to its minimum. On OLED displays, black pixels emit no light. Optional app indicators and Apple’s system privacy indicators can still be visible; this does not switch off or lock the device.

While the screen is black

The camera remains ready, and an active video recording continues while the app stays in the foreground. An optional dim red “REC” indicator confirms recording, and a faint “Hold to wake” hint helps you return to the controls. You can hide these two app indicators in Settings. Apple’s camera and microphone privacy indicators remain visible.

Waking the screen

Press and hold anywhere for about a second. A quick tap won't do it — that's on purpose, so a stray touch can't light up your screen mid-shot.

Black out automatically when recording

For Pro and existing Basic subscribers, the default is to black out the preview when recording starts. Change this in Settings → Screen Off → “Black screen when recording starts.”

Red night-vision mode

Tap the moon icon to tint the interface red and dim the display. This gives you a less bright interface for framing and adjusting a shot around a telescope or other photographers. Choose a brightness that suits the setting.

Manual controls Pro

With Blackout Pro, tap the sliders icon to open the manual panel:

  • ISO — set sensitivity manually across your camera's full range.
  • Shutter — set the exposure time, up to the longest your device supports, for brighter low-light frames.
  • Focus — set focus manually, or use the ∞ (infinity) shortcut as a starting point for distant subjects. Check focus on your scene; a lens-position shortcut does not guarantee sharp stars.
  • White balance — dial in color temperature by hand, or leave it on auto and watch it track the scene.
  • Night — brightens automatic exposure and allows a longer exposure within your camera’s limits. It does not force maximum ISO or shutter duration. Check the result and adjust exposure and focus for your scene.

Tap AUTO on any control to hand it back to the camera.

Focus, exposure & zoom

These gestures work for everyone, free:

  • Tap to focus on any point. A small brightness control (a sun) appears beside the focus box — drag it up or down to fine-tune the exposure. It works like a roller, so a few small drags give you precise control, and it resets each time you pick a new spot.
  • Press and hold the preview for about half a second to lock focus and exposure (AE/AF Lock) so they don't drift as the scene moves; a label confirms the lock. Tap anywhere to release.
  • Zoom by pinching or by sliding the on-screen zoom scale — drag left to zoom in, like the stock camera. The lens chips switch between your device's physical cameras.

You can turn the brightness control and press-and-hold lock on or off in Settings → Focus & Exposure. On devices with a supported hardware Camera Control, you can map it to zoom and available adjustments; manual controls require Pro.

Photo & video

Photo capture and video up to 1080p at 30 fps are free. In Settings → Photo, you can mirror front-camera selfies; Pro adds higher-resolution stills, up to 48 MP, and Apple ProRAW on supported cameras. Pro also adds video up to 4K at 60 fps, additional stabilization options, ProRes, Apple Log, and HDR. Each capability depends on your device, selected lens and recording configuration. Check the active capture format shown in the app. Choose Apple Photos, the In-App Library, the Private Folder on Pro, or a combination in Settings → Saving & Privacy → Save to. Apple Photos saving uses “Add Only” access and does not grant access to your existing library.

Audio

Open Settings → Audio to choose how video sound is recorded — all free:

  • Channels — mono, stereo, or spatial sound where the device and recording configuration support it. Unavailable formats fall back to a supported configuration.
  • Sample rate — Auto, 44.1 kHz, or 48 kHz.
  • Microphone — record from the iPhone mic or any connected source (AirPods, Bluetooth, or a wired/USB mic). If your chosen mic disconnects, the app switches back to the iPhone mic automatically.
  • Level meter — turn on an on-screen audio meter to keep an eye on your levels while recording.

Your in-app Library

Tap the thumbnail in the bottom-left corner to open the Library — captures saved to the In-App Library, grouped by day with a photo/video filter. Tap an item for a full-screen viewer with pinch-to-zoom, video playback, sharing and file information. Swipe up for the info panel, or down to close. The Library manages the app’s own copies, separately from Apple Photos. Photos-only captures are in Apple Photos; Private Folder captures require opening that folder. Removing a Library item does not remove another copy from Apple Photos or the Private Folder.

Where your captures are saved

Open Settings → Saving & Privacy and tick a combination of destinations under Save to. The app attempts each selected destination and reports a saving problem if one fails. At least one destination must stay on:

  • Apple Photos — your device's photo library, the same place the built-in Camera saves to. The app uses “Add Only” access, so it can add new captures but cannot see your existing photos.
  • In-App Library — the app's own copy, browsable on the Library screen. Removing something here never touches Apple Photos.
  • Private Folder Pro — a locked folder inside the app. See below.

Saving to multiple destinations can use additional storage. In version 1.5.8, both the In-App Library and Private Folder are eligible for device backup, including captures already stored by earlier versions. A completed backup depends on your Apple settings, available space and backup status; it is not guaranteed. Deleting the app removes its local copies. Export important files before deleting it, and check your backup separately.

Private Folder Pro

Blackout Pro includes a Private Folder inside the app. On a device with a passcode configured, opening it requires Face ID, Touch ID, or your device passcode. iOS performs authentication. The folder locks when you close it or the app enters the background.

It leads the Library screen as a labelled card, and it is also reachable from Settings → Saving & Privacy. You can move existing captures in or out of it from the Library — moving in takes the file with it rather than leaving a copy behind in the unlocked Library.

  • Hidden means only there. When the Private Folder is the only destination you have ticked, the capture is never added to Apple Photos and no preview appears on the camera screen's thumbnail button. If you also tick Apple Photos or the In-App Library, the same capture exists in an unlocked place too — so it is not hidden. The app says this in Settings as you tick the boxes.
  • Check every copy. A private capture may be your only copy, or another copy may exist in Apple Photos, the In-App Library, an export or a backup. Deleting the private item removes that local copy; it does not delete those other copies. The folder is eligible for device backup, subject to your Apple settings and a successful backup. Deleting the app removes its local contents.
  • If your subscription lapses, you keep your files. Opening the folder, viewing, exporting and moving existing files out remain available. Saving new captures there requires Pro. If Pro ends, the app removes the Private Folder from future save destinations and keeps your other selected destinations. If Private Folder was the only destination, it falls back to the In-App Library.
  • A device passcode is required for the lock. Without one, the folder cannot be locked. The app warns you and lets you reach your existing media. Set a passcode in your device’s Face ID & Passcode or Touch ID & Passcode settings to enable authentication.

Quick record — widget & Control Center

Add the Blackout Camera widget to your Home Screen, or a supported control to Control Center or the Lock Screen, to open the app and start recording. By default it uses your current camera settings; set separate quality options in Settings → Quick Record. Required permissions must be granted and paid features require an active entitlement. Keep the app open while recording; the shortcut does not enable background recording.

Location

With Save location data on (Settings → Metadata), your photos and videos are geotagged like the built-in Camera. A small GPS indicator near the top of the camera shows when a location fix is ready, and the in-app Library's info panel shows the coordinates and place for each capture. Location is only added when you allow it, and only to your own captures.

Tips for night-sky photography

  • Use a tripod or rest the phone on something steady — long exposures need a still camera.
  • With Pro, try the Night preset or adjust ISO, shutter time and focus manually. Check the preview and a test capture rather than assuming one setting fits every scene.
  • Turn off the flash and torch.
  • Use Screen Off when available to reduce preview glow. Recording behind the black preview requires Pro or an existing Basic subscription; Apple’s privacy indicators remain visible.
  • Give your eyes time to dark-adapt, and use red night-vision mode when you do need the screen.

FAQ

Does the camera really keep recording when the screen is black?

Yes, with Pro or an existing Basic subscription. The black preview is a display change and an active video recording continues while the app remains in the foreground. Calls, system interruptions, low storage or leaving the app can interrupt capture. Check the saved clip afterward.

How do I bring the screen back?

Press and hold anywhere on the black screen for about a second. A single tap is ignored on purpose so you can't wake it by accident.

Can I just lock my phone instead?

No — locking the phone or pressing the side button suspends the app and stops the camera. Use the in-app Screen Off button instead, which keeps the camera running while the display is black. The app also keeps your phone from auto-locking while it's open.

Why does it need camera and microphone access?

The camera is needed to take photos and record video; the microphone adds sound to videos. The app processes captured media on your device and does not upload it to MW Supply Limited. You control save destinations and sharing; Apple Photos and device backups follow your Apple settings. See the Privacy Policy.

Why does my video sometimes have no sound?

If you record during a phone call, iOS gives the call exclusive use of the microphone, so the video records without audio. The app shows a dim red “No audio” warning while this happens, and the clip stays silent for its whole length even if the call ends partway through. End the call before recording to capture sound.

Where are my photos and videos saved?

Choose Apple Photos, the In-App Library, the Private Folder on Pro, or a combination in Settings → Saving & Privacy → Save to. Apple Photos is the default and uses “Add Only” access. At least one destination must stay selected. If saving fails, follow the app’s message and check the destination before deleting or moving any remaining copy. Deleting the app removes local Library and Private Folder files; export important files first. These folders are eligible for device backup in 1.5.8, but a backup must actually complete to protect a copy.

What is the Private Folder, and is a capture really hidden?

On a device with a passcode configured, the Private Folder is protected by Face ID, Touch ID or the device passcode. Without a device passcode it cannot be locked, and the app warns you. Saving only to the Private Folder does not add that capture to Apple Photos or the unlocked Library. If you select another destination too, or move a file that already has other copies, those copies remain outside the lock. Deleting a private item removes its local private copy. Existing private files remain accessible, including export and moving them out, if Pro ends; only new private saves require Pro.

The screen is very dark even after I wake it.

The app lowers display brightness in black-screen or red night-vision mode and restores the previous brightness when you leave those modes or the app backgrounds. Press and hold the black preview for about a second to wake it. If the display still seems too dark, check red night-vision mode and adjust brightness in Control Center.

How do I manage, restore, or cancel my subscription?

Open Settings → Membership in the app to see your plan, renewal date, and price, switch between monthly and annual, restore a purchase on a new device, or open Apple's subscription management. You can also manage or cancel any time in iOS Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions. Subscriptions renew automatically until cancelled; cancelling stops the next renewal and you keep access until the current period ends.

My subscription stopped working, or my payment failed.

Check Settings → Membership and your Apple Account subscription status. A declined renewal can enter Apple’s billing retry or grace period; access depends on the status Apple reports. The app shows a payment message when that state is available. Use Update Payment Method or Apple’s subscription settings to resolve payment problems; Restore Purchases does not repair a declined payment. Use Restore Purchases when a purchase on your Apple Account is missing from the app, and allow time for Apple’s status to refresh.

What happened to tokens?

Tokens were a legacy currency for black-screen recording and premium video options. Earning and sales ended in 1.5.5; versions 1.5.5–1.5.7 retained spending and synchronization of existing balances. Version 1.5.8 removes the token balance, spending and synchronization. An earlier token purchase does not become a subscription. Pro now provides these paid features; existing Basic subscribers keep their plan’s rights. For an earlier purchase, contact support or request a refund through Apple. Eligibility is determined by Apple and applicable consumer law.
