Blackout Camera

A camera for the dark. Shoot photos and video on iPhone and iPad while the screen stays completely black. Version 1.4.3

About

Blackout Camera is a professional camera built for dark environments — astrophotography, meteor showers, the aurora, long-exposure landscapes, observatories, night wildlife, and anywhere a glowing phone screen would ruin the moment or other people's shots. It records like the standard camera, but with one tap (or automatically the instant you start recording) the screen goes fully black while the camera keeps shooting. There's also a red night-vision mode and a full set of manual controls for serious low-light work. It's free to download and use for photos and video; the signature black-screen mode and the pro tools are described under Plans, tokens & ads below.

The big idea: Press Screen Off and the display turns black and dims to nothing — but the camera keeps recording. To bring the screen back, press and hold anywhere for half a second. The hold is deliberate so the screen can't wake by accident on a tripod or in your pocket.

Getting started

  • Allow camera access the first time you open the app (and microphone, the first time you record video).
  • Choose PHOTO or VIDEO with the selector above the shutter.
  • Tap the shutter to take a photo or start/stop recording.
  • Tap Screen Off any time to black out the display while shooting.
  • Your photos and videos are saved straight to your Photos library.
  • Tap the thumbnail in the bottom-left corner to open your in-app Library and browse everything you've shot.
  • Add the Home Screen widget (or the Control Center / Lock Screen control) to start a black-screen recording instantly.

The black screen

Turning it on

Tap the Screen Off button. The interface disappears, the screen turns black, and screen brightness drops to its minimum so the display emits as little light as possible. On OLED iPhones, black pixels are effectively switched off.

While the screen is black

The camera keeps doing exactly what it was doing — a photo session stays ready, and a video recording keeps rolling. If you like, a very dim red “REC” dot confirms a recording is in progress, and a faint “Hold to wake” hint sits at the bottom. Both can be turned off in Settings for total darkness.

Waking the screen

Press and hold anywhere for about half a second. A quick tap won't do it — that's on purpose, so a stray touch can't light up your screen mid-shot.

Black out automatically when recording

By default, starting a video recording blacks the screen immediately. You can turn this off in Settings → Screen Off → “Black screen when recording starts.”

Red night-vision mode

Tap the moon icon to wash the entire interface in deep red and dim the screen. Red light preserves your eyes' dark adaptation far better than white or blue light, so you can glance at the screen to frame or adjust a shot without losing your night vision — useful at the telescope or around other astrophotographers.

Manual controls Pro

With Blackout Pro, tap the sliders icon to open the manual panel:

  • ISO — set sensitivity manually across your camera's full range.
  • Shutter — set the exposure time, up to the longest your device supports, for brighter low-light frames.
  • Focus — lock focus manually, with a one-tap ∞ (infinity) button for sharp stars. Each control shows its live value at all times — even on automatic.
  • White balance — dial in color temperature by hand, or leave it on auto and watch it track the scene.
  • Night — one tap brightens the automatic exposure and lets the camera use a longer exposure to gather more light in very dark scenes, without ever blowing out to white. Pair it with the ∞ button for the stars.

Tap AUTO on any control to hand it back to the camera.

Focus, exposure & zoom

These gestures work for everyone, free:

  • Tap to focus on any point. A small brightness control (a sun) appears beside the focus box — drag it up or down to fine-tune the exposure. It works like a roller, so a few small drags give you precise control, and it resets each time you pick a new spot.
  • Press and hold the preview for about half a second to lock focus and exposure (AE/AF Lock) so they don't drift as the scene moves; a label confirms the lock. Tap anywhere to release.
  • Zoom by pinching or by sliding the on-screen zoom scale — drag left to zoom in, like the stock camera. The lens chips switch between your device's physical cameras.

You can turn the brightness control and press-and-hold lock on or off in Settings → Focus & Exposure. On iPhone 16 and later you can map the hardware Camera Control to zoom and your manual adjustments.

Photo & video

Photos use Apple's computational photography, and in Settings → Photo you can choose 12 MP or your sensor's maximum resolution (up to 48 MP on supported iPhones), capture Apple ProRAW (a high-bit-depth DNG) where your device supports it, and mirror front-camera selfies — all free. Video records up to 4K at 60 fps with multiple cinematic stabilization levels. On Pro iPhones, Blackout Pro unlocks ProRes, Apple Log, and HDR capture where your device supports them. Everything is saved to your Photos library using “Add Only” access, so the app never reads your existing library.

Audio

Open Settings → Audio to choose how video sound is recorded — all free:

  • Channels — mono, stereo, or spatial sound (spatial needs a supported device, such as iPhone 16 and later, and falls back to stereo elsewhere).
  • Sample rate — Auto, 44.1 kHz, or 48 kHz.
  • Microphone — record from the iPhone mic or any connected source (AirPods, Bluetooth, or a wired/USB mic). If your chosen mic disconnects, the app switches back to the iPhone mic automatically.
  • Level meter — turn on an on-screen audio meter to keep an eye on your levels while recording.

Your in-app Library

Tap the thumbnail in the bottom-left corner to open the Library — a gallery of everything you've captured with Blackout Camera, grouped by day with a photo/video filter. Tap any item for a full-screen viewer with pinch-to-zoom, video playback, share, and per-item info (date, size, resolution, duration, and location). Swipe up for the info panel, swipe down to close. The Library keeps the app's own copies of your captures, separate from Apple Photos — so removing something here only removes the app's copy and never touches your Photos library.

Quick record — widget & Control Center

Add the Blackout Camera widget to your Home Screen, or the control to Control Center or the Lock Screen, and one tap opens the app and starts a black-screen recording immediately — ideal for pocket-to-recording moments. By default it uses your current camera settings; you can give quick recordings their own resolution, frame rate, and stabilization in Settings → Quick Record.

Location

With Save location data on (Settings → Metadata), your photos and videos are geotagged like the built-in Camera. A small GPS indicator near the top of the camera shows when a location fix is ready, and the in-app Library's info panel shows the coordinates and place for each capture. Location is only added when you allow it, and only to your own captures.

Tips for night-sky photography

  • Use a tripod or rest the phone on something steady — long exposures need a still camera.
  • Open manual controls and tap Night, or set focus to ∞ and raise ISO and shutter time yourself.
  • Turn off the flash and torch.
  • Start the shot, then tap Screen Off (or let it black out automatically) so your screen doesn't fog up other people's long exposures.
  • Give your eyes time to dark-adapt, and use red night-vision mode when you do need the screen.

FAQ

Does the camera really keep recording when the screen is black?

Yes. The screen going black is purely a display change — the camera session keeps running and video keeps recording the whole time.

How do I bring the screen back?

Press and hold anywhere on the black screen for about half a second. A single tap is ignored on purpose so you can't wake it by accident.

Can I just lock my phone instead?

No — locking the phone or pressing the side button suspends the app and stops the camera. Use the in-app Screen Off button instead, which keeps the camera running while the display is black. The app also keeps your phone from auto-locking while it's open.

Why does it need camera and microphone access?

The camera is needed to take photos and record video; the microphone is needed to record sound with your videos. Nothing is ever uploaded — see the Privacy Policy.

Why does my video sometimes have no sound?

If you record during a phone call, iOS gives the call exclusive use of the microphone, so the video records without audio. The app shows a dim red “No audio” warning while this happens, and the clip stays silent for its whole length even if the call ends partway through. End the call before recording to capture sound.

Where are my photos and videos saved?

Directly to your device's Photos library. The app uses “Add Only” access, so it can save new captures but cannot see your existing photos.

The screen is very dark even after I wake it.

Blackout Camera lowers screen brightness while you're in black or night-vision mode and restores it when you leave. If brightness ever seems stuck, exit the app and adjust brightness in Control Center; it is always restored when you close the app.

How do I manage, restore, or cancel my subscription?

Open Settings → Membership in the app to see your plan, renewal date, and price, switch between monthly and annual, restore a purchase on a new device, or open Apple's subscription management. You can also manage or cancel any time in iOS Settings → [your name] → Subscriptions. Subscriptions renew automatically until cancelled; cancelling stops the next renewal and you keep access until the current period ends.

What are tokens, and do they expire?

Tokens are an in-app currency the free tier uses to unlock the black-screen mode and premium video options per session. They don't expire. Earn them free by watching an optional ad or claiming the daily bonus, or buy a pack. Subscribers don't need tokens — Basic and Pro include the black-screen mode unlimited. Tokens are consumable and non-refundable once spent.
