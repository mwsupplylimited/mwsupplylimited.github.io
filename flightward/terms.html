4. Data, advisories and coverage

Aeronautical, terrain and weather data changes constantly and the sources Flightward draws on contain errors, gaps and stale records. In particular:

  • Airspace geometry is simplified so it can be searched in flight; boundaries are approximations, not the published boundaries.
  • Airspace advisories cover the United States and a hand-curated Hong Kong dataset only. Elsewhere, airspace advisories are inactive and the app says so. An area with no airspace drawn is not evidence of clear airspace.
  • Weather, TFR, ATIS and, where offered, NOTAM information comes from third-party services that can be delayed, throttled, or unavailable, and plain-English decodes are convenience translations shown beside the source text, which always governs.
  • Terrain, obstacle and elevation data is coarse, incomplete, and never a substitute for seeing and avoiding.
  • Charts and data packs expire. Every chart, weather product, plate and airspace layer carries an issue or valid time, and it is your responsibility to read it and to keep the app's data current.
  • Position and altitude come from the device or from equipment you own. GPS altitude is geometric and will disagree with your altimeter and with the altitude ATC sees. A phone's barometer reads cabin pressure. Any of these inputs can be absent, degraded or wrong without warning.
  • Aural advisories are deliberately suppressed at low height and during high-workload phases so that they cannot distract you. Silence never means "clear".

5. Weight, balance, fuel and performance

Weight-and-balance, fuel, density-altitude and performance results in Flightward are arithmetic performed on numbers that you entered. They are not manufacturer-approved performance data, they are not derived from any authoritative aircraft database, and they carry no approval. Flightward ships no authoritative-looking aircraft defaults; the figures come from your aircraft's approved flight manual, entered by you, and it is your responsibility to enter them correctly, to keep them current, and to verify every result against that manual before flight.
